What is the Internet?

The Internet is a global system of all interconnected computer networks, it allows collaboration and communication between individuals and their computers regardless of their geographic locations through communication protocols especially TCP/IP. It is one of the most successful revolutions and investments that the World has ever seen.

Milestones of the History of the Internet

The Launch of ARPANET - 1969

The roots of the modern Internet began with the Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA) in the late 1960s. ARPANET was created by ARPA to establish communication between research centers and universities for the purpose of sharing resources at different locations through a packet-switched network. The first ARPANET message was sent on October 29, 1969, allowing computers at UCLA and Stanford Research Institute to communicate

DARPA - ARPANET

The idea of an Open-Architecture Networking - 1972

Researchers wanted to go beyond simple communication and communicate across different networks instead of connecting through one. This led to the idea of an open architecture introduced by Robert Kahn in 1972 helped by Vint Cerf also known as “internetworking”, that allows connections between networks, each designed and developed differently, having their own unique interface depending on their users’ requirements and specific environment.

Internet Society, A Brief History of the Internet

Earlier Transmission Control Program - 1974

In 1974 RFC 675 specifying the Transmission control program was published by Vint Cerf, Yogen Dalal and Carl Sunshine transforming the internetworking from a simple idea into a technical protocol. It explained how communication could happen between interconnected networks.

RFC Editor - RFC 675

ARPANET Switches to TCP/IP - 1983

One of the most interesting challenges of ARPANET was switching from NCP to TCP/IP Protocol on January 1, 1983. This transition had been carefully planned by the networking community for several years and went more smoothly than expected. This revolutionary change allowed computers to communicate over multiple independent networks. Nowadays TCP/IP is still the core protocol suite used by the Internet today.

Internet Society - A Brief History of the Internet

Introduction of the Domain Name System (DNS) - 1983

In November 1983, the Domain Name System (DNS) was introduced through RFC 882 and RFC 883. DNS simplified communication by allowing users to use easy-to-remember domain names instead of memorizing IP addresses, while the system automatically translates the domain name into the corresponding IP address.

RFC Editor - RFC 882 RFC Editor - RFC 883

NSFNET-1986

U.S National Science Foundation launched NSFNET in 1986 which was one of the most significant TCP/IP-based network connecting academic researchers to a new system of supercomputer centers. At first only 2000 computers were connected, later on the NSFNET expanded rapidly, reaching more than 2 million computers connections in the 1990s.

National Science Foundation - Internet / NSFNET

Decommission of ARPANET - 1990

By January 1983, ARPANET had become part of a larger network of networks, which essentially formed the Internet. As the Internet continued to grow, larger networks took over much of ARPANET’s role, which became less necessary. It was officially decommissioned in 1990. This shows how rapidly the Internet grew after the development of interconnections between multiple networks.

Internet Society - A Brief History of the Internet

Decommission of NSFNET - 1995

Commercial firms decided to take advantage of the Internet to expand. They began creating their own network infrastructures, leading them to provide their own Internet services. This expansion led to the shutdown of the NSFNET backbone in 1995.

National Science Foundation - Internet / NSFNET

Creation of ICANN – 1998

The Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers (ICANN) was created as a non-profit organization with global participation in 1998. Its main role was to help coordinate domain names, as websites and online services were expanding rapidly and needed to be properly managed so that users could reach them.

ICANN - History

Launch of DNSSEC – 2010

In the 2000s, the Internet became a major source of communication and interaction between people, As the Internet became increasingly important, protecting DNS information from modification and false responses became necessary. On July 15, 2010, the DNS root zone was signed using DNSSEC (Domain Name System Security Extensions). Its role was to make sure that DNS information was authentic and had not been modified during communication.

IANA - Root DNSSEC Launch

World IPv6 Launch – 2012

As the years passed, more and more devices started to connect to the Internet, and IPv4 addresses started to run out, meaning that a new type of IP with a larger address space was needed. IPv6 already existed before 2012. What happened on June 6, 2012 was the World IPv6 Launch, organized by the Internet Society, when many companies permanently enabled IPv6, representing a major improvement for the continued growth of the Internet.

Internet Society - World IPv6 Launch

QUIC Standardization – 2021

Today’s internet applications require fast and secure connections between the client and the server. The QUIC protocol version 1 was standardized in May 2021 by the Internet Engineering Task Force through RFC 9000. The goal of the QUIC protocol is to enable secure connections, fast connection establishment, support for multiple streams of data, and path management.

RFC Editor - RFC 9000 (QUIC)

key Technologies

Packet Switching

Packet switching is an important technology used in computer networks. In the 1960s, researchers such as Leonard Kleinrock worked on this idea. A 1965 experiment showed that computers could communicate over long distances, but normal telephone systems were not good for this type of communication. Packet switching solved this problem by dividing data into small parts called packets. These packets can travel through different paths in the network and then be put back together at the destination.

TCP/IP

TCP/IP mainly refers to two protocols:

  1. IP
    An Internet protocol(IP) is made to deliver a packet of data from a source to its destination across interconnected networks
  2. TCP
    Transmission control protocol provides reliable data transfer. It detects data loss and errors, and corrects them through retransmission.

TCP/IP is revolutionary because it allows different networks to communicate with each other reliably, making the Internet possible.

Domain Name Space

DNS is a system that translates domain names, like google.com, into IP addresses that computers can understand. Before DNS, host names and their corresponding addresses were maintained through centralized lists, which became difficult to manage as the network grew. DNS introduced a distributed system that automatically resolves domain names into IP addresses.
